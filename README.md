## Resources for Learning Data Science
**Dataset:**
I chose the [St. Louis Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED)](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/) from the [Awesome Data Science list](https://github.com/academic/awesome-datascience) GitHub.

**Description:** The FRED database includes a collection of economic datasets maintained by the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis. These various datasets include economic indicators such as real gdp, unemployment rate, cpi, gold, etc. FRED datasets have many potential applications for all industries. It is used widely in economic research to study relationships between economic indicators over time. Economists also study the relationship between outside variables and these indicators through regression analyses. FRED datasets can also be utilized by many businesses to track economic trends in order to assist their decisions. 


**Data Science Book:**
I chose the [Advanced Data Analysis from an Elementary Point of View](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~cshalizi/ADAfaEPoV/ADAfaEPoV.pdf) book written by Cosma Rohilla Shalizi. Cosma is an Associate Professor of Statistics at Carnegie Mellon University whose work focuses on ["prediction and inference for dependent, and often high-dimensional, data."](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~cshalizi/) I found this book on the [Free Data Science Books](https://github.com/chaconnewu/free-data-science-books) GitHub.

**Why I Chose This Book:** 
I chose this book because I have always been very interested in data analysis and enjoy finding insights and predictions from a given dataset. This book covers topics ranging from simple regression to causal inference and simulation-based inference. I think it would be a fantastic resource to learn a lot about data analysis and how to conduct it.
